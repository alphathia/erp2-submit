> As generative AI tools like ChatGPT become embedded in technical education, a critical challenge emerges: how can we ensure these tools foster learning rather than bypass it? This study provides empirical evidence that pedagogical design, not merely model access, determines the educational value of AI assistants.


## P002

- **Section:** 1 Introduction

- **Relevance:** benefit

- **Text:**

> Students using the structured assistant scored an average of 92.7 on the culminating project, significantly outperforming peers in the unstructured condition (mean = 74.3, p < .001). Gains were particularly strong in areas requiring abstraction, justification, and documentation, which are skills critical across software engineering, IT, and cybersecurity.


## P003

- **Section:** 3.1 Prompt Sequence Framework

- **Relevance:** interaction_mode

- **Text:**

> Our assistant uses a four-phase conversational scaffold: (1) Explain – Students describe a concept or intent using their own language. (2) Predict – They forecast the behavior or outcome of a decision. (3) Reflect – After executing or observing, they revisit their belief and compare it to what occurred. (4) Revise – Students update their reasoning or approach based on structured questioning.
