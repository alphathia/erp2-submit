## P001

- **Section:** The strategic dance: from socratic inquiry to deadline driven delegation

- **Relevance:** usage_pattern

- **Text:**

> At the start, I try to use it to understand things. I have time, so I ask it to explain concepts I’m shaky on. But when it’s 2 AM and the project is due at 9 AM... I’m not trying to learn anymore, I’m just trying to finish. I just ask it for the code I need to make it work. It’s about survival at that point.


## P002

- **Section:** Partnership framing

- **Relevance:** interaction_mode

- **Text:**

> It felt like having a senior programmer sitting right next to me. When I got stuck, I would explain the problem to it, and we would work through the problem together. It’s much faster than trying to find an answer on your own.


## P003

- **Section:** The Novice’s Dilemma: trust but can’t verify

- **Relevance:** challenge

- **Text:**

> I know Copilot can be wrong... but half the time, the code it gives me is more complex than what I would write, so I don’t even know how to check if it’s right. It uses some weird Java stream thing, and I just have to cross my fingers and run it. It’s stressful because you don’t know if you’re learning the right way or just making a bigger mess.


## P004

- **Section:** The boilerplate blindspot

- **Relevance:** challenge

- **Text:**

> It was so frustrating. I spent two hours debugging my complex scheduling algorithm, convinced the logic was wrong somewhere. But the error was actually in the simple CSV parsing code that Copilot wrote at the beginning. I didn’t even look at it because, I mean, it’s just reading a file. I just assumed it would be correct.
